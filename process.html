How it works

Ice to vapour, never liquid.

Flash Frozen Curing is a patented method: fresh cannabis is flash frozen and then dried under vacuum using temperature, time and pressure. No solvents or chemicals are ever used.

Below the triple point of water, 611.657 pascals, ice cannot melt. It can only leave as vapour, and that is how over 80% of the water leaves the flower.

For adults 21+.

Behind this page: NASA’s Space Power Facility vacuum chamber in 1970, with a Skylab shroud inside it. NASA, public domain.

  1. Harvest

    The plant is harvested and the flower is taken off the stem.

  2. Freezer

    Frozen within an hour of harvest. It waits as ice until it is dried.

  3. Vacuum

    In the chamber the frozen water goes straight from ice to vapour and is pulled away. Only temperature, time and pressure.

  4. Jar

    Over 80% of the water is gone. With the water’s weight out of it, a gram fills the jar like two, and the bud breaks up by hand.

Deck 02The record

The record

Four issued US patents.

All titled “Method of drying botanicals”, with Eric Young as the inventor, and all four trace back to one provisional application filed on 18 June 2018. The first was granted on 5 May 2020. They are public record at the US Patent and Trademark Office.

Deck 03Keeping it

Keeping it

Three things keep it right.

Keep it sealed. Air is the enemy of the texture. Keep it cool. A cupboard, not a windowsill. Keep it dark. Light works on flower the way it works on anything green.

That’s it. No humidity packs, no fussing. And it is meant to break up by hand: just pinch, break, and roll.

Engine room

Why a vacuum.

In a vacuum, ice does not melt. It leaves as vapour.

That is the whole method: cold first, then a vacuum, and the ice goes out as vapour without melting.
