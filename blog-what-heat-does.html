What does heat do to flower as it dries?

Heat dries flower quickly, and it takes two things with it. The aroma compounds, the terpenes, evaporate along with the water, and the cannabinoids begin to convert from the acid forms the plant makes. The hotter the drying, the more of both. Drying without the heat keeps more of each.

The hotter, the less aroma

Terpenes are small, light molecules, which is why they reach the nose. It is also why heat drives them off.

A 2021 study of hemp dried flower with hot air and infrared heat at temperatures from 40 to 90 °C. The share of terpenes retained fell from 82.1% at the gentle end to 29.9% at the hot end.

A study of a high-THC cultivar found the same direction. Every drying method lost some terpenes, and drying at 80 °C left about half as much as the gentler settings. In a study of hemp flowers, the gentlest method kept about 85% of the aroma oil and the harsher ones kept between 52 and 76%.

Heat converts the cannabinoids

The plant makes its cannabinoids in an acid form, such as THCA and CBDA. Heat converts them, and it starts during drying.

In the 2021 study, the share of CBDA converted rose from 0.2% to 14.1% as the drying temperature went up. That study also freeze-dried some hemp for comparison, and a 2022 review reports that it converted less than the hemp dried with hot air.

Drying without the heat

Researchers at McGill University measured the terpenes in one cannabis variety three ways. Fresh, it held 4.3 grams per 100 grams of dry matter. Freeze-dried, it held 3.9. Dried with microwave-assisted hot air, it held 2.8. THC and THCA were unchanged by freeze-drying.

No method keeps everything, and the lightest aroma compounds are the first to leave under any of them. Slow drying at room temperature is gentle as well. What these studies agree on is that heat is where the losses climb.

What it means for Space Weed

Flash Frozen Curing uses temperature, time and pressure. Space Weed is frozen within an hour of harvest, and the water leaves as vapor from ice, under vacuum, not in a stream of hot air.
