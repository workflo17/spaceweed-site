How does freeze-drying take the water out of flower?

Freeze-drying removes water as vapor, straight from ice. The flower is frozen first, then held under a vacuum so the ice never melts on its way out. The water leaves without ever being liquid and without being cooked off, so the flower keeps most of the shape it had on the plant.

Ice that never melts

Water can be ice, liquid or vapor, and which one it is depends on temperature and pressure. There is a single combination, called the triple point, where all three can exist together: 0.01 °C at a pressure of 0.612 kilopascals, which is well under one percent of the pressure of the air around us.

Below that pressure, liquid water cannot exist. Ice that is warmed gently does not melt. It turns straight to vapor. The name for this is sublimation, and a 2020 review of freeze-drying in plant foods sets out the physics.

What a freeze dryer does

A freeze dryer puts that to work in three steps.

  1. The material is frozen solid.
  2. The chamber is pumped down to a vacuum below the triple point.
  3. The ice is given just enough warmth to sublimate, and the vapor is drawn off and caught.

With no liquid water and low temperatures, most of the reactions that degrade a product stop. A standard review of the method makes that point about high-value foods.

Why it is not the usual way

It is the expensive way to dry anything. The 2020 review puts the energy used at four to ten times that of hot-air drying. That is the reason it has mostly been kept for high-value foods.

What it means for Space Weed

Flash Frozen Curing is CannaGenesis’s process for doing this to flower. Space Weed is frozen within an hour of harvest and then dried under vacuum, using temperature, time and pressure. Over 80% of the water leaves.

The method is set out in four issued US patents, each titled “Method of drying botanicals”. The first was granted in 2020.
