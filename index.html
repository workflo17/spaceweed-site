Cargo bay · the one thing

2x more bud. Same weight.

Most cannabis is dried by hanging for weeks. Space Weed is frozen within an hour of harvest and dried under vacuum.

Over 80% of the water leaves. What’s left is the flower, with the water’s weight gone. That is why the jar looks twice as full, and why the bud breaks up by hand.

Engine room · how it works

Ice to vapour, never liquid.

In a vacuum, ice skips melting and goes straight to vapour.

Below the triple point of water, 611.657 pascals, ice cannot melt. That is how over 80% of the water leaves the flower. The comet in the window is doing the same thing: sunlight turns its ice straight to vapour.

The window: comet 67P, photographed by ESA’s Rosetta on 10 September 2015. Sunlight warms the comet’s ice and the ice turns straight to vapour, carrying dust out in jets. Slowed, looped and tinted in processing.

Engine room · the four stages

  1. HarvestThe flower comes off the stem.
  2. FreezerFrozen within the hour. It waits as ice until it is dried under vacuum.
  3. VacuumIce leaves as vapour. Only temperature, time and pressure.
  4. JarA gram fills the jar like two.

Four issued US patents.

All four are titled “Method of drying botanicals”, with Eric Young as the inventor, and all four trace back to one provisional application filed on 18 June 2018. The first was granted on 5 May 2020. They are public record at the US Patent and Trademark Office.

Observation deck · ship’s log

Why it’s called Space Weed.

We got our name from our early supporters, who compared the texture of our Flash Frozen bud to astronaut ice cream. The process happens in a vacuum, and most of the flower’s water leaves as vapour.

We experimented with temperature, pressure and timing until we had a method. It became Flash Frozen Cured.
